# SportNinja vs BLH Internal Memo

**Internal only**
**Audience:** BMHL-style large adult hockey leagues
**Checked:** March 13, 2026

## Summary

The honest read is simple: SportNinja almost certainly wins the first-glance pricing reaction because its public pricing is transparent, seat-based, and monthly. For a league comparing websites side by side, that immediately feels lower-risk and easier to budget.

That does **not** mean BLH should respond by trying to out-argue their public sticker price. The better sales position is:

- acknowledge that SportNinja looks cheaper at first glance
- explain why the comparison is incomplete for commissioner-led adult hockey leagues
- reframe around operational fit, migration friction, payment-model flexibility, and long-term league control

For BMHL-style leagues, BLH should position itself as the better fit when the buyer cares about:

- commissioner operations beyond scoring
- flexible first-season commercial structuring
- mixed payment workflows during transition
- branded public league presence
- migration support and backup/data confidence

## 1. Public Pricing Comparison

### SportNinja public pricing

Based on [SportNinja Pricing](https://www.sportninja.com/pricing) and [SportNinja FAQ](https://www.sportninja.com/faq):

| Plan | Public price | Public limit / note |
|---|---|---|
| Free | `$0/month` | up to `50 participants` |
| Pro | `$50/month` | up to `500 participants` |
| Ultimate | `$100/month` | up to `500 participants` |
| Enterprise | Custom | white-label, `min. 10K participants or pro leagues` |

Public FAQ/payment notes:
- billing is monthly
- active participants count toward billing
- admins, team reps, and officials count as participants
- payment gateway transaction fees still apply

### BLH current public pricing posture

Based on current BLH live pricing copy:

- `First season partner pricing as low as 1.5%`
- flexible year-one pricing for qualified leagues
- structured around league size, payment mix, and onboarding needs

This is intentionally less explicit than SportNinja's public pricing.

### What leagues hear

#### SportNinja
`This is affordable, clear, and easy to compare.`

#### BLH
`This is custom, so it may be more expensive or more complicated.`

### What that usually means in practice

#### Why SportNinja feels safer immediately
- the pricing page reduces comparison friction
- the monthly number looks approachable
- buyers can explain it internally without a call

#### Why that comparison is incomplete
- participant-based pricing is not the same as commissioner-operation pricing
- the payment provider and transaction fee story still exists
- public sticker price does not tell you how the platform handles mixed payment realities, migration effort, or deep commissioner workflows

#### What BLH should say
- `You are right that SportNinja looks cheaper at first glance.`
- `Where BLH is different is that we scope year one around how your league actually operates, especially when you are moving off spreadsheets, e-transfer, or an older website stack.`
- `We are not trying to be the easiest public pricing page. We are trying to be the better operating system for leagues with more moving parts.`

## 2. Capability Comparison by Buying Criteria

### Scoring, Live Game Flow, and Stats

### SportNinja public position
- Strongest public positioning area
- Public site leads with official digital scoring, real-time stats, live schedules, standings, and participant engagement
- Public help content supports official/scoring workflows and officials setup

### BLH current position
- Available in BLH today
- Strong enough to compete if demoed well
- Should not be sold as an abstract parity claim without a live walkthrough

### Who this matters to
- Leagues where scorekeepers, officials, and player engagement are central
- Buyers who think `scoring system first, everything else second`

### How to talk about it on a call
- `SportNinja clearly leads with scoring in public. We should respect that.`

## 3. BMHL-Specific Objection Handling

### `SportNinja is cheaper`

`At first glance, that is a fair reaction. Their public pricing is clearer and easier to compare. Where BLH becomes stronger is when the league needs more than a low-friction monthly sticker price. If your real challenge is commissioner operations, migration, mixed payments, and getting the whole league off a patchwork stack, that is the conversation BLH is built for.`

### `We mostly need scoring and payments`

`If that is truly the whole requirement, SportNinja is a fair comparison. But BMHL is already describing problems around commissioners, team movement, payments, websites, migration, and bookkeeping pressure. That moves this beyond a simple scoring-and-payments tool decision.`

### `We still use some e-transfer`

`That is not a side detail. That is exactly why year-one structure matters. BLH is a better conversation when the league is trying to transition into cleaner digital operations without pretending every payment will immediately move online.`

### `We need our books to stay clean`

`That concern is valid and we should treat it seriously. The right move is not to say 'don't worry about it.' The right move is to show the actual payment model, reconciliation approach, and where the operational visibility lives. That is a stronger answer than just pointing to a cheaper public subscription.`

### `We do not want a painful migration`

`Then the cheapest public pricing page is not automatically the safest platform decision. BLH should frame the decision around switching risk, migration support, and whether the system can handle the league's current reality instead of requiring a perfect reset.`

### `We need access to our data if something happens to your company`

`That is a legitimate board-level question, and BLH has a good answer for it. We should say clearly that backup/export access is part of the operating model, not an afterthought. That reduces the platform-risk objection more effectively than a generic reassurance.`

## 4. Red / Yellow / Green Positioning Guidance

### Green
- Commissioner-first operations
- Flexible first-season commercial structuring
- Branded public league websites
- Mixed payment workflows during transition
- Migration support and backup/data-access posture

### Yellow
- Live scoring/statistics parity claims
- Bookkeeping/export claims unless the exact workflow is already demoable
- Advanced staff/ref payroll automation unless the exact flow is proven

### Red
- Do not claim BLH is always cheaper
- Do not imply all leagues qualify for `1.5%`
- Do not overstate white-label/API parity unless specifically confirmed

## 5. Commercial Recommendations

### 1. How BLH should answer the `cheaper` objection

Use this structure:

1. agree with the surface-level read
2. explain why the comparison is incomplete
3. re-anchor on commissioner fit, migration friction, and payment-model reality

Recommended line:

`SportNinja may look cheaper on the public pricing page, and that is a fair first reaction. BLH is the stronger fit when the league needs a commissioner operating system, a safer migration path, and a payment model that reflects how the league actually runs.`

### 2. What pricing message BLH should use for large switching leagues

Recommended message:

`Flexible first-season partner pricing for qualified leagues, scoped around league size, payment mix, and onboarding needs.`

Why this works:
- it keeps `1.5%` as an upside, not a promise
- it supports the BMHL-style mixed-workflow conversation
- it avoids trying to out-simplify SportNinja on public pricing

### 3. What public pricing or sales-collateral improvement should come next

Best next addition:
- a commissioner-operations comparison sheet or slide

It should explain:
- why BLH does not use a simple participant-seat story
- what year-one transition pricing is meant to solve
- what buyers get beyond scoring and registration alone
