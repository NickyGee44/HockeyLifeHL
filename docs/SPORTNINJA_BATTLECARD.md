# SportNinja vs BLH Battlecard

**Internal only**
**Audience:** BMHL-style large adult hockey leagues
**Checked:** March 13, 2026

## Executive Takeaway

SportNinja likely wins the first-glance pricing comparison because its public pricing is simple, explicit, and monthly.

BLH should win when the buyer cares about commissioner operations, payment flexibility, migration support, branded public league presence, and hockey-specific workflow depth that goes beyond scoring alone.

## Public Pricing Comparison

| Topic | SportNinja public pricing | BLH public pricing |
|---|---|---|
| Entry point | Free: `$0/month` for up to `50 participants` | `First season partner pricing as low as 1.5%` |
| Mid-tier | Pro: `$50/month` for up to `500 participants` | Flexible year-one pricing for qualified leagues |
| Higher tier | Ultimate: `$100/month` for up to `500 participants` | Structured around league size, payment mix, and onboarding needs |
| Enterprise | Custom / white-label, `min. 10K participants or pro leagues` | Standard, First Season Partner, Association / Multi-League |
| Billing model | Monthly | Scoped commercial model |
| Participant counting | Public FAQ says active participants count, including admins, team reps, and officials | Public copy does not use a participant-seat model |
| Transaction fees | Public FAQ says payment gateway transaction fees still apply | BLH messaging is explicit that year-one structure depends on how the league actually collects money |

### What Leagues Hear

- SportNinja: `This is cheaper and easier to understand.`
- BLH: `This is more custom, so it might cost more.`

### What That Usually Means in Practice

- SportNinja wins the quick sticker-price reaction.
- SportNinja still requires a payment provider, still has transaction fees, and counts active participants at the account level.
- BLH is intentionally not publishing a one-size-fits-all rate because the leagues most likely to compare seriously are often switching from spreadsheets, mixed payments, e-transfer, old websites, and manual commissioner workflows.

### How We Should Answer

- Start by agreeing that SportNinja looks cheaper at first glance.
- Do not argue headline price against headline price.
- Reframe to year-one predictability, migration burden, payment-model flexibility, and commissioner operations depth.
- Use: `If you only want simple public pricing and a faster self-serve comparison, SportNinja will look cleaner. If you want the system to fit how your league actually runs, that is where BLH is stronger.`

## Capability Comparison by Buying Criteria

### 1. Scoring, Live Game Flow, and Stats
- **SportNinja public position:** Strong public emphasis on official digital scoring, real-time stats, standings, schedules, and a participant app.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today, but this should be sold carefully and demoed live rather than claimed abstractly.
- **Who this matters to:** Commissioners who see scoring and stats as the heart of player engagement.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `SportNinja clearly leads with scoring in its public message. We should only compete here by demo, not by broad claims.`

### 2. Registration and Payment Collection
- **SportNinja public position:** Publicly supports registration, payments, waivers, and integrated payment providers.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today, with stronger flexibility for leagues transitioning from mixed workflows instead of assuming a clean all-online model from day one.
- **Who this matters to:** Leagues still using a mix of card, e-transfer, and manual follow-up.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `If your registration flow is messy today, BLH is designed to meet you there instead of forcing a clean-slate operating model.`

### 3. Player Billing vs Team Billing vs Hybrid Billing
- **SportNinja public position:** Not publicly clear at the same level of detail.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today with individual, team, and hybrid billing paths.
- **Who this matters to:** Large adult leagues where captains, team balances, and partial/offline payment handling matter.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `This is one of the best BLH differentiators for adult hockey leagues with real-world payment complexity.`

### 4. Public League Websites, Branding, and Domain Control
- **SportNinja public position:** Publicly promotes branded websites, embedded widgets, and white-label enterprise options.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today with branded public league sites and custom domain support.
- **Who this matters to:** Leagues that want a serious public-facing presence, not just an app and admin tool.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `Both platforms talk about public presence. BLH should win when the buyer wants the website to feel like the league's real home, not just a companion surface.`

### 5. Migration and Onboarding Support
- **SportNinja public position:** Publicly says onboarding and training are included and smaller leagues can self-configure.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today with migration intake, uploaded source analysis, and league backup/export tooling.
- **Who this matters to:** Existing leagues with historical data, old websites, and admin fear around switching.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `We should lean hard into migration confidence and data portability. This is one of the strongest reasons not to reduce the conversation to sticker price.`

### 6. Commissioner Operations
- **SportNinja public position:** Publicly mentions suspensions, waivers, officials, role-based tooling, and tournament/bracket support.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today for suspensions/discipline, free-agent and team-placement workflows, playoffs, and official management. Some of this should still be demo-led.
- **Who this matters to:** Commissioners, league owners, and operations-heavy organizers.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `This is where BLH should frame itself as a commissioner operating system, not just a league app.`

### 7. Data Portability, Backup Access, and Admin Confidence
- **SportNinja public position:** Not publicly clear.
- **BLH current position:** Available in BLH today through league-owned backup exports and migration-center workflows.
- **Who this matters to:** Serious leagues worried about platform risk and long-term control.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `If the league asks what happens if we disappear, we have a better answer than most platforms: your data is exportable and your migration path is explicit.`

### 8. Bookkeeping and Reconciliation Readiness
- **SportNinja public position:** Publicly supports payment providers and notes transaction fees still apply, but bookkeeping/export depth is not especially clear.
- **BLH current position:** Stronger payment-model flexibility, but this should only be claimed where the workflow can be demoed.
- **Who this matters to:** Leagues with treasurers, accountants, or QuickBooks sensitivity.
- **How to talk about it on a call:** `Do not over-claim. Sell payment clarity and flexible ops first, then demo the accounting path you can actually stand behind.`

## BMHL-Specific Objection Handling

### `SportNinja is cheaper`
`At first glance, yes, it probably looks cheaper because the public pricing is simple and monthly. Where BLH is different is that we scope year one around how your league actually runs, especially if you're switching from spreadsheets, e-transfer, and manual admin. We are not trying to win a sticker-price argument. We are trying to make the switch workable and predictable.`

### `We mostly need scoring and payments`
`If that is truly the whole requirement, SportNinja is a fair comparison. Where BLH becomes stronger is when you also need commissioner control over registrations, team billing, hybrid billing, migration, website presence, and the operational realities around free agents, suspensions, and season setup.`

### `We still use some e-transfer`
`That is exactly the kind of league BLH is designed for in year one. We can support mixed payment workflows while you transition more of the league into the platform, instead of pretending every payment will flip to online overnight.`

### `We need our books to stay clean`
`That is a legitimate concern. We should not hand-wave it. The right way to position BLH is that we can structure the payment flow around your actual collection model, and then walk through the reconciliation path directly instead of promising magic bookkeeping.`

### `We do not want a painful migration`
`Then the question is not just who has the cheaper monthly price. The question is who gives you the safer path off your current stack. BLH should lean into migration support, uploaded source analysis, and a practical rollout plan instead of asking you to clean everything up first.`

### `We need access to our data if something happens to your company`
`That is a fair ask, and we should answer it directly. BLH has a stronger story here than most league software because we have explicit export and backup paths. This is one of the areas where we should be more confident, not less.`

## Red / Yellow / Green Guidance

### Green
- Commissioner-first operations
- Flexible first-season commercial structuring
- Branded public league websites
- Mixed payment workflows during transition
- Migration support and backup/data-access posture

### Yellow
- Live scoring/statistics parity claims
- Bookkeeping/export claims unless the exact workflow is demoable
- Advanced staff/ref payroll automation unless the exact flow is proven

### Red
- Do not claim BLH is always cheaper
- Do not imply all leagues qualify for `1.5%`
- Do not overstate white-label/API parity unless specifically confirmed

## Commercial Recommendations

1. **Answer the cheaper objection honestly**
   - Use: `SportNinja may look cheaper on the public pricing page. BLH is a better fit when the league needs commissioner operations, migration support, and payment flexibility, not just an easier sticker price.`

2. **Use one consistent pricing message for large switching leagues**
   - Use: `Flexible first-season partner pricing for qualified leagues, scoped around league size, payment mix, and onboarding needs.`

3. **Improve the sales collateral next**
   - Add a commissioner-operations comparison slide or one-pager that explains why BLH does not reduce the conversation to participant seats and monthly sticker price.
